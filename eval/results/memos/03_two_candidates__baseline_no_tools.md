# Site-Screening Memo: Fort Bend County, TX vs. DeKalb County, GA

*Prepared from memory without live data access. Figures are approximate and should be verified before use in any approval document.*

## Recommendation

**Pursue Fort Bend County first, conditionally, with DeKalb as a second-priority or opportunistic option.**

- **Fort Bend** offers more developable land, a deregulated power market with a defined (if long) large-load process, and no state income tax. Its main negatives are hurricane and flood exposure, grid reliability optics after Beryl and Uri, and an ERCOT large-load queue that makes energization timing the key unknown.
- **DeKalb** is an inner-suburban, largely built-out county adjacent to the Atlanta market. Large contiguous parcels are scarce, and I recall the county adopting a data center moratorium or restrictive zoning review in 2025 (verify status). Georgia Power's large-load pipeline is also heavily subscribed. DeKalb only becomes attractive if you can buy an already-entitled, already-powered site or shell.
- **What would flip this:** if a Fort Bend parcel cannot get a credible utility energization date inside your window (CenterPoint and ERCOT study results), or if DeKalb offers a specific entitled site with a signed Georgia Power service commitment.

## County notes

**Fort Bend County**
- The best sites are likely on the county's west and south sides, where parcels are larger and floodplain risk is manageable. Check each tract against FEMA maps and Harvey inundation data.
- CenterPoint is the likely T&D provider for most of the county. Confirm the specific service territory per parcel, since some areas are served by other utilities or municipal systems.
- ERCOT's process lets you proceed without a vertically integrated utility's capacity commitment. The tradeoff is that you carry market price and curtailment risk, so on-site generation or storage and a hedged PPA strategy matter.
- Reliability and resilience design (flood elevation, fuel storage, ride-through for hurricanes) will add cost and should be in the budget from the start.

**DeKalb County**
- Strengths are proximity to Atlanta's interconnection ecosystem, Georgia's talent base, and lower catastrophic climate risk.
- Constraints are land scarcity, neighborhood opposition, the reported moratorium, and a regulated utility that is prioritizing and rationing large loads.
- Realistic plays are a powered shell, a brownfield or industrial conversion, or a site with a prior utility load study. A greenfield multi-hundred-MW campus is unlikely here. Adjacent counties (Douglas, Fayette, Newton, Rockdale) may be better for that.

## Risks and unknowns

1. **Power timing (both).** Neither county's energization date can be confirmed from public information. This is the dominant risk.
2. **ERCOT rule changes.** SB 6 implementation, including curtailment and cost-sharing, is still evolving and could change economics for loads at your scale.
3. **Georgia Power large-load tariff terms.** Minimum bills, contract length, and collateral requirements could materially change cost.
4. **DeKalb moratorium status.** I am not certain of the current scope, duration, or exemptions.
5. **Hurricane and flood exposure in Fort Bend.** This affects design cost, insurance, and customer perception.
6. **Water.** Cooling approach (air or hybrid versus evaporative) will determine local feasibility in both counties.
7. **My figures.** Prices, queue sizes, and market sizes are rough and dated. Treat them as screening-grade only.
